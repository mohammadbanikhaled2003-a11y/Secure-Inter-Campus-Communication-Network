🛡️ Four Explicit Trust Boundaries

  • TB0 (Offline Root CA Realm): Air-gapped root authority protected with passphrase and isolated from networks and containers.
  • TB1 (HQ Protected Perimeter): High-trust internal network hosting sensitive academic databases and ticket services.
  • TB2 (Branch Perimeter): Authenticated local employee subnet requiring encrypted inter-campus transit.
  • TB3 (Untrusted WAN Transit): Zero-trust network assumed to be fully compromised by active adversaries.

Slide 3: Two-Tier PKI Architecture & Revocation Policy

Strict Profile Enforcement & Mitigation of the Soft-Fail Revocation Flaw

🌳 Two-Tier CA Hierarchy

  • Root CA (Offline): ECDSA P-384, 5-year validity, pathlen:1 constraint. Used exclusively for one-time signing of Issuing CA.
  • Issuing CA (Online): ECDSA P-384, 2-year validity, pathlen:0 constraint. Signs end-entity leaf certificates and publishes signed CRL.
  • Certificate Profiles:
    • Portal: serverAuth with SAN portal.g06.scp.lab.
    • Gateways: serverAuth, clientAuth, 1.3.6.1.5.5.7.3.17 (ipsecIKE).
    • Staff: clientAuth with personal verified email SAN.

🚫 Hard-Fail Revocation Mandate

  • The "Soft-Fail" Flaw: If CRL download fails or is actively blocked by an attacker, vulnerable validators proceed and accept revoked certificates.
  • Our Engineering Decision: We mandate Hard-Fail in Nginx and strongSwan. If the certificate cannot be validated against a valid, unexpired CRL, it is immediately rejected.
  • Public Distribution: CRL published over plain HTTP at http://pki.g06.scp.lab/crl/issuing.crl to prevent recursive TLS deadlocks (RFC 5280).
💬 Speaker Note: "We eliminated single-tier CA risks and prevented MITM attacks through ECDSA P-384 keys, path length constraints, and strict Hard-Fail CRL checking."
